# Product design brief

Status: high-fidelity direction approved for implementation

## Confirmed direction

Use a **Notion-style application design with Attio-like polish**.

The product should feel like a calm, intelligent working document rather than a
traditional marketing dashboard or influencer marketplace. It should combine
Notion's clarity and familiarity with the sharper typography, spacing, and
customer-data presentation shown in the supplied Attio reference.

### Visual character

- Light interface for the first release.
- Warm white or very light neutral page background.
- Near-black text with a quiet gray hierarchy.
- Mostly monochrome controls with one strong pink signature accent.
- Bold, confident page titles paired with highly readable body text.
- Thin borders, minimal shadows, and softly rounded corners.
- Simple outline icons and very little decorative illustration.
- Generous whitespace around page structure, with compact spacing inside data
  tables and review panels.
- Subtle grid or document-canvas cues may be used sparingly, particularly on
  empty states, onboarding, and brand overview pages.

Primary typeface: Inter.

### Interaction character

- Familiar left sidebar similar to a workspace/document tool.
- Breadcrumbs and clear page titles instead of oversized dashboard chrome.
- Tables for phrases, creators, runs, shortlists, and publications.
- Cards only when they communicate a distinct object such as a brand, content
  asset, match explanation, or evidence item.
- Inline editing where it is safe and obvious.
- Full pages for substantial research and review; drawers for quick supporting
  detail.
- Quiet status pills and restrained progress indicators.
- Sticky review controls where long evidence pages require a decision.
- Almost no animation beyond short, functional transitions and loading states.

### Product tone

The application should feel:

- focused;
- editorial;
- precise;
- trustworthy;
- premium without being luxurious;
- powerful without feeling technical or intimidating.

It should not feel:

- like a colorful influencer marketplace;
- like an enterprise analytics dashboard full of charts;
- overly rounded, glossy, gradient-heavy, or playful;
- crowded with provider terminology;
- like a generic component-library demo.

### Initial component language

- Page radius: approximately 8-10px for cards and inputs.
- Borders: 1px neutral gray.
- Shadows: none by default; very soft elevation only for menus and drawers.
- Buttons: compact, near-black primary; white secondary with a gray border;
  quiet text actions for low-priority controls.
- Inputs: document-like, clean, and easy to edit rather than heavy form boxes.
- Tables: sticky headings where useful, comfortable row height, subtle hover,
  visible sorting and filtering, and no unnecessary grid lines.
- Tags: small neutral pills; color reserved for semantic meaning.
- Empty states: useful instruction and one clear action, not large illustrations.

### Confirmed accent color

Use a vivid warm pink based on the supplied reference:

- Signature pink: `#FF2D5F`
- Accessible dark pink: `#E8174F`
- Soft pink tint: `#FFF0F4`
- Deep pink text/accent: `#A80D38`

The bright signature pink supplies the product's personality. Use it for active
navigation markers, selected states, highlights, progress, focus rings, small
graphic moments, and occasional large feature surfaces.

Use the darker pink for primary buttons with small white text. White on the
bright signature pink should be limited to large or bold text; use near-black
text where smaller text appears on the bright pink. The soft tint is suitable
for selected table rows, evidence highlights, tags, and quiet empty states.

Pink should remain distinctive rather than becoming background decoration. Most
pages stay warm white, charcoal, and neutral gray, with roughly 5-10% of the
visible interface using the accent. Semantic success, warning, and error colors
remain separate and should not be replaced by pink.

### Key review-page layout

The creator–content match review should use a three-part workspace:

1. A compact creator identity and eligibility summary.
2. The matched brand asset, target question, explanation, and evidence stream.
3. A sticky decision area containing impact score, accept/maybe/reject, notes,
   shortlist selection, and proposed collaboration angles.

The evidence—not the AI score—should occupy the most visual space.

### Reference supplied

Attio marketing/product visual reference: monochrome palette, strong modern
headings, generous spacing, fine borders, calm layout, and compact dark primary
actions. Use this as directional inspiration, not as a page to copy.

You do not need to use design terminology. Screenshots, links, rough sketches,
and reactions such as “I like the spacing but hate the colors” are all useful.
